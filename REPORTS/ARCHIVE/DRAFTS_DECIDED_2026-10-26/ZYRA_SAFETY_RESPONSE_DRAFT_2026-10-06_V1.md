[Illegal-content duties](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/illegal-content-duties-under-the-online-safety-act?language=en), [assessment and record-keeping guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/check-how-to-comply-with-the-illegal-content-rules).

### 3.2 NCII: current measures are not a blanket technology instruction

Ofcom opened an NCII enforcement programme on **9 September 2026**; the relevant new Code measures took effect **30 September**. Its focus includes hash matching or alternative measures used to satisfy applicable duties. This is a current issue, not a future launch-only concern. [Enforcement programme](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/Enforcement-programme-into-measures-being-taken-by-services-to-prevent-users-from-encountering-or-sharing-non-consensual-intimate-imagery-NCII?language=en).

The issued user-to-user Code, ICU C14.1–C14.2, has specific service/type/scale/risk conditions and defines relevant content by reference to public communication. For example, its high-risk limb includes pornography-purpose services, services above 700,000 monthly active UK users, or file-storage/file-sharing services; another limb covers large services at medium/high risk. This packet **does not decide ZYRA's classification or C14 applicability**, require every small app to buy hash matching, or treat exclusion from one Code measure as exemption from the underlying safety duties. Document the actual facts, relevant measures and justified alternatives. [Issued Code, 9 September, pp.32–33 / PDF pages 31–32](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/information-for-industry/illegal-harms/detecting-intimate-image-abuse/illegal-content-codes-of-practice-for-user-to-user-services-9sep2026.pdf?v=425251).

Where used, hash matching needs quality assurance, error correction and proportionate human review informed by performance and harm. It is not a substitute for victim reporting or a reliable indication that unmatched content is safe. Privacy duties still apply. No provider, hash database, scanning of member content, new processing purpose or automatic sanction is authorised here. [Ofcom human-review guidance, 9 September 2026](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/information-for-industry/illegal-harms/detecting-intimate-image-abuse/guidance-on-appropriate-proportion-of-human-review-for-intimate-image-abuse-hash-matching.pdf?v=425050).

### 3.3 CSEA, privacy complaints and breach clocks

- CSEA is a separate urgent branch, not ordinary adult NCII handling. Ofcom's guidance (updated 23 July) describes the reporting duty effective for regulated user-to-user services from 7 April 2026, including detected/unreported content, the operator's UK position and an appropriate NCA/equivalent reporting route. The duty is not itself a requirement to detect all content proactively. Registration, statutory reporting timeframes/retention, duplicate-report handling and trained human authority require explicit preparation and approval; none is performed here. [CSEA guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/duty-to-report-child-sexual-exploitation-and-abuse-csea-content-know-the-rules-and-how-to-comply).
- A data-protection complaint needs a clear route, acknowledgement within **30 days**, investigation and updates/outcome without undue delay. Thirty days is not permission to leave an urgent safety issue untriaged, nor a substitute for a rights-request deadline. [ICO complaints guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/how-to-deal-with-data-protection-complaints/), [ICO commencement notice, 23 June 2026](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/06/new-data-protection-complaints-law-now-in-force/).
- A suspected personal-data breach needs immediate human assessment, recorded awareness time and a reasoned notification decision. Notifiable UK GDPR breaches require notification without undue delay and within **72 hours where feasible**; high-risk affected-person notification is without undue delay. Incomplete facts are not a reason to wait for a perfect investigation. Every breach needs a record, including a justified no-notification decision. No existing vulnerability is declared an actual breach by this packet. [ICO breach guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/personal-data-breach/personal-data-breaches-a-guide/).
- Rights requests require their own due-date and exemption assessment; requests and complaints can coexist. Erasure is not absolute, but there is no blanket safety exception to keep everything. [ICO erasure guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/right-to-erasure/).

## 7. Complaints, appeals, rights and incident conflicts

**Complaints/appeals proposal.** Provide a route for a failure to act, an incorrect restriction/removal, unsafe disclosure or unsatisfactory handling. Distinguish safety reporting, a service complaint, a moderation appeal and a data-protection complaint; one message can initiate several tracks without forcing repeated submission. Do not reset the original receipt or statutory clock on reassignment. A status UI is informational, not an appeal decision or assurance that someone is on duty.

**Rights/deletion proposal.** Verify identity proportionately; a Guardian is not automatically authorised to request another adult's private data.
